FINAL REVIEW AND ACCEPTANCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Student:
Does the final ADR make the rejected alternatives and the reasons for the
decision clear? Does the overview help? Are the painful parts visible?

Coding assistant:
Yes. The final ADR makes the trade-off explicit:

* A layered monolith is simpler, but is more likely to develop accidental
  coupling unless stronger module boundaries are imposed.
* Microservices offer independent scaling, but introduce distributed
  transactions, sagas, duplicate/out-of-order events, service security,
  observability, and deployment overhead that the requirements do not justify.
* The modular monolith preserves a single transaction boundary for inventory,
  orders, and ticket issuance while isolating provider integrations and
  creating a path for later extraction.

The overview table helps a reader compare consistency, operations, scaling,
and future evolution at a glance. The diagrams show how normal requests and
the risky purchase flow move through the system. The ADR also makes the
painful parts visible: concurrency control, webhook idempotency, outbox
retries, authorization, secure credentials, and recovery from partial
failures.

The final ADR is saved as `ADR-001-event-ticket-modular-monolith.md`.
